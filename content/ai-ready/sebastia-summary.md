## 1. Short overview
A multi-period hill site north-west of Nablus whose layers run from the Iron Age city widely identified with ancient Samaria, through Roman Sebaste, to Byzantine, Crusader and Ottoman remains.

## 2. Key historical timeline
| Period | Event |
| --- | --- |
| 9th century BCE | Occupation begins; in the Iron Age the site is a prominent regional political centre. |
| Hellenistic period | The city becomes a fortified stronghold. |
| Roman period | Herod renames the city Sebaste. |
| Roman period (Septimius Severus) | Sebaste receives the status of a Roman colony. |
| Byzantine period | The city declines in importance but becomes the seat of a bishopric. |
| Crusader era | Notable Crusader-period structures are added. |
| 2026 | Inscribed on the World Heritage List and on the List of World Heritage in Danger. |

## 3. Cultural and religious importance
UNESCO stresses how legible the successive layers are. Unlike many continuously inhabited cities, where later building hides earlier remains, Sebastia keeps relatively direct traces of each major period, so its political, religious and urban history can be read in one place.

## 4. Main landmarks
- Iron Age remains of a regional political centre
- Hellenistic fortifications
- Remains of the Roman city of Sebaste
- Byzantine and Crusader-period vestiges
- Ottoman-era layers


## 5. Important facts
- Occupation here is documented from the 9th century BCE. The city was a fortified stronghold in the Hellenistic period, was renamed Sebaste by Herod and became a Roman colony under Septimius Severus; in the Byzantine period it was the seat of a bishopric. UNESCO inscribed Sebastia on the World Heritage List in 2026 and placed it on the List of World Heritage in Danger the same year; its statement names land expropriation plans aimed at dividing the property among the threats to its integrity.
