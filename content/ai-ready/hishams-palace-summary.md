## 1. Short overview
An Umayyad winter retreat about 4 km north of Jericho, built between 724 and 743 AD and famous for its bath-hall mosaics and the Tree of Life.

## 2. Key historical timeline
| Period | Event |
| --- | --- |
| 724–743 AD | Built in the Umayyad period, most likely by Caliph Hisham bin Abd al-Malik. |
| 743–744 AD | Decorated by Hisham’s heir, al-Walid II. |

## 3. Cultural and religious importance
Palestine’s tentative-list entry calls it one of the most significant early Islamic sites in Palestine. Its mosaics, stucco, frescoes and carved stone blend Byzantine and Sasanian traditions with Coptic and Roman designs, and it is a rare example of human and animal figures in Umayyad secular art. The palace’s star-shaped rosette window has become a symbol of Jericho.

## 4. Main landmarks
- The two-storey palace around an arcaded courtyard
- The great audience hall and bath with 38 mosaic carpets
- The diwan and its Tree of Life mosaic
- The rosette (star) window, a symbol of Jericho
- The mosques and the fountain pavilion in the forecourt
- The Northern Area estate with its large grape press


## 5. Important facts
- Inscriptions suggest it was built by Caliph Hisham bin Abd al-Malik and decorated by his heir al-Walid II; an earthquake destroyed it in 749 AD. The bath hall’s floor, about 30 by 30 metres, is made of 38 mosaic carpets — one of the largest early Islamic mosaic floors in the world. The first excavations, from 1935 to 1948, were directed by Dimitri Baramki.

## 9. Visitor walkthrough
Along the west side of a shared forecourt stood the palace, a mosque and the audience hall with its bath, around a pool under a pavilion. In the north-west corner of the hall, the small diwan held the finest mosaic, the Tree of Life. Water arrived by an open channel from the ‘Ein Duk and ‘Ein Nu’eima springs at the foot of Mount Quruntul, five kilometres to the west.
