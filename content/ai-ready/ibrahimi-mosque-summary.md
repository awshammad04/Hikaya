## 1. Short overview
A sacred architectural complex at the heart of Hebron's old town.

## 5. Important facts
- The monumental enclosure around the Cave of Machpelah (al-Ghar al-Sharif) was built in the 1st century AD, probably by Herod, and the site has stayed in religious use from the early Roman period to today. The Hebron Rehabilitation Committee documents successive Byzantine, Islamic and Crusader architectural layers. The town's main roads lead to the mosque, and in the Ayyubid and Mamluk periods Sufi lodges (zawaya) spread through the quarters around it. Nearby, the Takiyya of Ibrahim, a hospice already described in 9th-century accounts, still offers meals to the poor and to visitors.

## 6. Religious traditions
- Religious tradition holds that the cave contains the tombs of Ibrahim / Abraham and the patriarchs and matriarchs of his family, and the site is venerated in Judaism, Christianity and Islam; this is presented as belief, not archaeological proof.
