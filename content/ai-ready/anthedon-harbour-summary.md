## 1. Short overview
The first known seaport of Gaza, called Tida or Blakhiyeh in Islamic literature, inhabited from 800 BC to 1100 AD.

## 2. Key historical timeline
| Period | Event |
| --- | --- |
| 800 BC | The earliest documented habitation of the harbour city. |
| 1100 AD | The end of the documented habitation. |
| 2012 | Submitted to Palestine’s Tentative List. |

## 3. Cultural and religious importance
Palestine’s tentative-list entry presents Anthedon as a clear example of the Eastern Mediterranean seaports on the ancient trade route between Europe and the Levant, with underwater heritage that has yet to be explored.

## 4. Main landmarks
- The mud-brick acropolis projecting towards the sea like a tower
- Remains of a Roman temple and a section of wall
- Roman artisan quarters and a series of villas
- Mosaic floors, warehouses and fortified structures
- Underwater archaeology off the shore


## 5. Important facts
- The harbour saw Neo-Assyrian, Babylonian, Persian, Greek, Roman, Byzantine and early Islamic rule. Excavations uncovered a Roman temple, villas, mosaic floors, warehouses, a 65-metre mud-brick wall and massive earthen walls 8 metres high and 6 metres thick. One kilometre to the south lay Maiumas, the ancient harbour of Gaza.
