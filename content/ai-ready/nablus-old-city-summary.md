## 1. Short overview
A dense Roman–Islamic old city between Mount Ebal and Mount Gerizim, with six main quarters, covered markets, bathhouses and the soap factories behind Nabulsi soap.

## 2. Key historical timeline
| Period | Event |
| --- | --- |
| 72 AD | The Roman city of Flavia Neapolis, “the new city”, is founded under Vespasian. |
| 636 AD | Under Muslim rule, Nablus becomes part of the district of Jund Filastin, with its capital at Ramla. |
| 1099 AD | Crusader control; the fortress and several churches are rebuilt on the eastern side. |
| 1187 AD | The Ayyubids regain the city; churches are restored and converted into mosques. |
| 1198 AD | A devastating earthquake is followed by reconstruction under the Ayyubids and Mamluks. |
| 1516 AD | Ottoman rule begins a period of major expansion; the 18th century becomes the city’s golden age. |
| 1882 | The city counts 30 soap factories, the backbone of its economy. |
| 1898 | The Clock Tower is built in Bab al-Saha, the main square. |
| 2024 | Nabulsi soap-making is inscribed on UNESCO’s Intangible Cultural Heritage list. |
| 2026 | The historic city is added to Palestine’s Tentative List. |

## 3. Cultural and religious importance
Known as “Little Damascus”, Nablus grew for twelve centuries as an Arab-Islamic centre of trade and culture while keeping its Roman street plan. The 18th century is often seen as its golden age, when mosques, markets, caravanserais, hammams and soap factories were built in the historic centre.

## 4. Main landmarks
- Six main quarters: al-Yasmina, al-Qaryoon, al-Qaysariyya, al-Habla, al-Gharb and al-‘Aqaba
- Bab al-Saha square, the Clock Tower (1898) and al-Nasr Grand Mosque
- The Old Khan and the New Khan covered markets
- Historic soap factories
- The Satoun Mosque and al-Salahi Grand Mosque
- Roman traces: the Decumanus, the nymphaeum and the al-Baidarah baths


## 5. Important facts
- Nablus stands on the Roman city of Flavia Neapolis, founded under Vespasian in 72 AD, and its two main streets still follow the Roman Decumanus and Cardo. The Ottoman Clock Tower in Bab al-Saha square was built in 1898. By 1882 the city had 30 soap factories, and Nabulsi soap-making was inscribed on UNESCO’s Intangible Cultural Heritage list in December 2024.

## 9. Visitor walkthrough
Two main streets, al-Nasr and Khan al-Tujjar, run east to west and divide the old city into three sections; al-Saha square lies at their junction. Residential quarters are reached through covered passages called dawamees, which open onto narrow alleys, stairways that follow the slope, small squares and family courtyards (ahwash).
