## 1. Short overview
The sacred mountain of the Samaritans above Nablus, whose summit holds a Hellenistic town, a Byzantine church and a later Muslim shrine.

## 2. Key historical timeline
| Period | Event |
| --- | --- |
| 128 BC | The Hellenistic Samaritan town on the summit is probably destroyed by John Hyrcanus. |
| 2nd century AD | A temple to Zeus is built on Tell er-Ras, overlooking Flavia Neapolis. |
| 484 AD | An octagonal church dedicated to Mary Theotokos is built on the main summit. |
| 529 AD | After the Samaritan revolt the church is turned into a fortress, later strengthened by Justinian. |
| 8th–9th centuries | The church is abandoned and the fortress dismantled. |

## 3. Cultural and religious importance
The Samaritans, today a small Palestinian community of a few hundred people, keep Mount Gerizim as their religious centre. Their village below the summit now includes a museum, and their Passover ceremony on the mountain continues a form of worship they believe to be thousands of years old.

## 4. Main landmarks
- The Hellenistic acropolis with its paved sacred precinct and casemate walls
- Remains of the octagonal church of Mary Theotokos (484 AD)
- The shrine of Sheikh Ghanim
- Tell er-Ras, site of a 2nd-century temple to Zeus
- The Samaritan village and its museum


## 5. Important facts
- The temple found on the summit existed before the later 2nd century BC; the town around it was probably destroyed by John Hyrcanus in 128 BC. In 484 AD an octagonal church dedicated to Mary Theotokos was built on the summit and later fortified under Justinian. In the 16th century a shrine of the Muslim saint Sheikh Ghanim was built on a corner of the ruined church.

## 6. Religious traditions
- In Samaritan belief, Mount Gerizim is the mountain of the Blessing and the site of the first temple in the Holy Land, built by Yosha’ bin Noun; Samaritans also believe that a rock on the summit is where Abraham was about to sacrifice his son Isaac.
