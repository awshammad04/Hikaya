## 1. Short overview
A historic urban ensemble shaped by Mamluk and Ottoman building traditions.

## 5. Important facts
- The old town spread over three hills and into the valley around the Ibrahimi Mosque, its focal point since the town moved down from Tell Rumeida after the Roman and Byzantine periods. Local limestone shaped its building in the Mamluk period (1250–1517), and it grew at a crossroads of caravan routes between southern Palestine, Sinai, eastern Jordan and the north of the Arabian Peninsula. Its houses cluster around small courtyards in the hosh system, and continuous “rampart houses” on its edges defended it in place of a town wall. Restoration with traditional materials since the mid-1990s has brought inhabitants back. UNESCO inscribed the old town in 2017 and placed it on the List of World Heritage in Danger the same year.
