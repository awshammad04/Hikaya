## 1. Short overview
The place identified by Christian tradition since the 2nd century as the birthplace of Jesus, where the oldest Christian church in daily use stands among its convents at the end of the pilgrimage route.

## 2. Key historical timeline
| Period | Event |
| --- | --- |
| 339 AD | The first church on the site, of the time of St Helena, is completed above the grotto. |
| 6th century (533 AD) | The church is rebuilt after a fire; this Justinian-era building is essentially the church standing today. |
| 12th century | Crusader-period additions, including the paintings on the columns, mark a new upsurge in pilgrimage. |
| 1878 | The Status Quo of the Holy Places, under which the three churches share the site, is set by the Treaty of Berlin. |
| 2012 | Inscribed on the World Heritage List and on the List of World Heritage in Danger. |
| 2019 | Removed from the List of World Heritage in Danger. |

## 3. Cultural and religious importance
UNESCO describes the church as an outstanding example of an early church within a remarkable architectural ensemble. It reflects two stages of history: the conversion of the Roman Empire to Christianity in the 4th–6th centuries, and the Crusader period that embellished the church and developed the three convents around it. Bethlehem has been a pilgrim destination for some 1,700 years, and the site is holy to Christians as well as to Muslims.

## 4. Main landmarks
- The Church of the Nativity basilica
- The grotto venerated by Christian tradition as the birthplace
- The Armenian, Franciscan and Greek Orthodox convents
- The Church of St Catherine (Custody of the Holy Land)
- Star Street and the historic gate of Qos al-Zarara
- Manger Square


## 5. Important facts
- A first church was completed here in 339 AD. The present church is essentially the 6th-century rebuilding of the time of Justinian; it keeps parts of the original 4th-century floor mosaics and has 12th-century additions. UNESCO inscribed the property in 2012, and it stayed on the List of World Heritage in Danger from 2012 to 2019. The Greek Orthodox Church, the Custody of the Holy Land and the Armenian Church share it under the Status Quo of the Holy Places.

## 6. Religious traditions
- According to Christian tradition, a cave beneath the church is where Jesus was born; belief in this place is recorded from at least the 2nd century AD.

## 9. Visitor walkthrough
The pilgrimage route that UNESCO describes starts near King David’s Wells at the traditional entrance to Bethlehem. It follows Star Street through the old town gate of Qos al-Zarara to Manger Square in front of the church. Every Christmas the patriarchs of the three churches walk it ceremonially, recalling the path that Christian tradition gives to Joseph and Mary.
