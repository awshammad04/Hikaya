## 1. Short overview
A Greek Orthodox monastery carved into the eastern cliffs of the Mount of Temptation, looking over Jericho and the Jordan Valley.

## 2. Key historical timeline

| 2026 | Included in Palestine’s Tentative List within the Monasteries of the Jerusalem Wilderness (El-Bariyah). |

## 3. Cultural and religious importance
Palestine’s tentative list selects the monastery as one of four that represent the desert monastic tradition of the Jerusalem Wilderness. Its vertical, cliffside building, small chapels, cells and narrow corridors follow Byzantine monastic principles and suit the ascetic life of its monks.

## 4. Main landmarks
- The cave venerated as the place of Jesus’s fast
- Chapels and cells carved into the rock face
- Old paths up the cliff from Jericho and along Wadi el-Mafjar
- Restored aqueducts and cisterns of the Douka fortress


## 5. Important facts
- A monastery was built in the 6th century over the cave associated with the Temptation, and the site is also identified with Chariton’s second foundation, the Douka monastery. About forty caves on the mountain’s eastern slopes served monks as sanctuaries. The present monastery was restored in 1892 and is overseen by the Greek Orthodox Patriarchate of Jerusalem.

## 6. Religious traditions
- According to Christian tradition, drawing on the Gospel of Luke, Jesus fasted and meditated here for forty days and nights before Satan tried to tempt him.
