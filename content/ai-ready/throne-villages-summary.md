## 1. Short overview
Hilltop seats of the sheikhs who ruled the central highlands in the 18th and 19th centuries, where fortified mansions rise above the village houses.

## 2. Key historical timeline
| Period | Event |
| --- | --- |

| 2013 | The throne villages are submitted to Palestine’s Tentative List. |

## 3. Cultural and religious importance
The entry describes the throne villages as a distinctive architectural horizon of the central highlands and an exceptional testimony to the power of local leaders in the late Ottoman period. Allied to urban notables, the sheikhs built mansions closer to city architecture than to peasant houses.

## 4. Main landmarks
- Sheikhs’ residences on hilltops above smaller village houses
- High fortified walls and monumental entrances with decorated wooden gates
- Courtyards and open terraces
- The bent, L-shaped entrance hall that gave privacy to the women of the sheikh’s family


## 5. Important facts
- In the 18th and 19th centuries the central highlands were divided into twenty-four sheikhdoms. Each sheikh collected taxes for the Ottoman government, and the villages where the sheikhs and their clans lived were called “throne villages”. Ras Karkar and Deir Ghassaneh are two of the thirteen villages named in Palestine’s tentative-list entry.
