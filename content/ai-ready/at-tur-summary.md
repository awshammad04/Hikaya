# At-Tur and Mount of Olives — AI-Ready Summary
## 1. Short Overview
At-Tur is a Palestinian Jerusalem neighbourhood on the Mount of Olives ridge east of the Old City. Homes, schools, hospitals and shops share the ridge with churches, shrines and cemeteries. Its viewpoints make Jerusalem's topography unusually legible: ridge, Kidron Valley, walls and sacred precinct can be understood in one panorama.

## 2. Key Historical Timeline
| Period | What happened |
|---|---|
| Ancient and late antique periods | The ridge served as an eastern approach to Jerusalem and a place of burial, worship and cultivation. |
| 4th century CE | Christian pilgrimage buildings were established on the Mount of Olives and near Gethsemane in the Byzantine period. |
| Crusader period (1099–1187 CE) | Churches and pilgrimage routes were rebuilt or reorganised around sites identified in Christian tradition. |
| Ottoman era (1517–1917 CE) | The Palestinian village community and multiple religious institutions continued across the ridge. |
| 1924 CE | The present Basilica of the Agony/Church of All Nations at Gethsemane was consecrated; Antonio Barluzzi designed it above earlier remains. |
| Modern era | At-Tur developed into a dense Jerusalem neighbourhood while retaining major sacred sites, cemeteries and panoramic viewpoints. |

## 3. Cultural and Religious Importance
The ridge is important to Christian, Muslim and Jewish traditions and is also a living Palestinian neighbourhood. It cannot be reduced to a panorama: local schools, hospitals, houses, cafés and daily traffic form part of its heritage.

## 4. Main Landmarks
- Panoramic viewpoints toward the Old City
- Gethsemane and the Church of All Nations
- Tombs of the Prophets, identified by tradition
- Historic cemeteries
- At-Tur neighbourhood streets and institutions

## 5. Important Facts
- The Kidron Valley separates the Mount of Olives from the Old City.
- The present Church of All Nations was consecrated in 1924 and designed by Antonio Barluzzi.
- Its façade, mosaic pediment and dark interior were designed to support a solemn devotional atmosphere.
- The panorama reveals the physical relation among ridge, valley, walls and the Haram al-Sharif/Al-Aqsa compound.

## 6. Religious Traditions
- Identifications of tombs, the place of Jesus's prayer and other sacred points vary by religious tradition and must be attributed.

## 7. Oral Heritage and Popular Stories

## 9. Visitor Walkthrough
Start at a public overlook and orient yourself before taking photographs. Trace the Old City wall, then drop your eye into the Kidron Valley and back up the Mount of Olives. The Dome of the Rock is a powerful landmark, but also notice gates, cemetery terraces, domes, bell towers and the way pale stone changes colour with the sun. This single view explains why the ridge mattered as approach, burial ground and place of worship.

Descend only by a route suitable for your mobility—the gradient is steep and polished paving can be slippery. At Gethsemane, notice ancient-looking olive trunks while avoiding unsupported claims about the exact age of individual trees. On the Church of All Nations façade, look for columns and the large mosaic pediment; inside, dark blue vaults and subdued light create a deliberate contrast with the bright valley outside. Earlier remains beneath the modern church connect the 1924 building with a much longer pilgrimage history.

Do not miss At-Tur as a neighbourhood. Step away from the viewpoint long enough to notice schools, groceries, hospital traffic, cafés and residents navigating daily life around pilgrimage buses. Carry water, protect yourself from sun, dress respectfully for sanctuaries and cemeteries, and verify current opening and access conditions locally.
