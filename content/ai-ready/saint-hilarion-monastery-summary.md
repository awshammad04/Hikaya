## 1. Short overview
The ruins of one of the earliest monastic sites in the Middle East, founded in the 4th century by Saint Hilarion on the coastal dunes of Nuseirat.

## 2. Key historical timeline
| Period | Event |
| --- | --- |
| 4th century | Saint Hilarion founds the monastery as a mud-brick hermitage. |
| 4th–5th centuries | A thriving communal (cenobitic) monastery is documented. |
| 4th–7th centuries | The complex grows around the saint’s memory, with churches, baths and a pilgrim hostel. |
| 2024 | Inscribed on the World Heritage List and on the List of World Heritage in Danger. |

## 3. Cultural and religious importance
The monastery stood at a crossroads of trade routes between Asia and Africa and drew scholars and monks from across the Byzantine Empire, forming the community known as the Monastic School of Gaza. It records the move from solitary hermits to organised communities, and the meeting of monastic life and pilgrimage.

## 4. Main landmarks
- Three superimposed churches with mosaic floors
- Crypts and monks’ cells
- The refectory
- The water management system
- Baths within the pilgrim hostel


## 5. Important facts
- It was the first monastic community in the Holy Land, growing from a mud-brick hermitage into a large church complex. Its remains include three superimposed churches with elaborate mosaic floors, crypts, cells, a refectory, a water system and baths within a pilgrim hostel. UNESCO inscribed it on the World Heritage List in 2024 and, the same year, on the List of World Heritage in Danger.
