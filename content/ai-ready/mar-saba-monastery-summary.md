## 1. Short overview
A Greek Orthodox monastery built into the cliffs of the Kidron Valley, founded in 483 AD by Saint Sabbas and inhabited almost continuously ever since.

## 3. Cultural and religious importance
Palestine’s tentative-list entry describes Mar Saba as one of the oldest continually occupied monasteries in the world, and as the place where St Sabbas codified the Typikon that laid the groundwork for the Byzantine Rite. Its golden age came in the 9th and 10th centuries, when it drew theologians, musicians, poets and historians.

## 4. Main landmarks
- The Church of the Theotokos (502 AD), where the relics of St Sabbas lie
- The Church of Theoktistos, later St Nicholas (491 AD)
- More than 100 hermit cells, including the retreat of St John of Damascus
- The 6th-century Justinian Tower, 18 metres high


## 5. Important facts
- Mar Saba began as a line of hermit caves along more than two kilometres of cliffs, and many of its chapels and cells are carved into the limestone. St John of Damascus lived here in the 8th century. A severe earthquake damaged the monastery in 1834, and it was rebuilt largely with Russian support. It is overseen by the Greek Orthodox Patriarchate of Jerusalem; women are not permitted inside the monastery itself.

## 9. Visitor walkthrough
Most of the chapels, cells and shared rooms are cut directly into the cliff face of the Kidron Valley. The monks live without electricity or running water, light olive-oil lamps at night and draw water from a natural spring.
