## 1. Short overview
The Yasser Arafat Museum at Al-Muqata'a in Ramallah presents contemporary Palestinian national memory through Arafat's life and the Palestinian national movement. It is connected to the historic compound associated with his headquarters, office and residence, and overlooks his temporary mausoleum and the square where Palestinians gathered for his farewell.

## 3. Cultural and religious importance
The museum describes itself as a cultural and educational institution dedicated to preserving Arafat's legacy and presenting the history of the Palestinian national movement through his life. Its collections, programs and research facilities connect personal biography with contemporary national memory.

## 4. Main landmarks
- The main exhibition route of four ascending ramps
- The bridge linking the new museum building to the historic Muqata'a building
- The preserved former headquarters, office and residence area
- The Resource Centre and its library and bilingual electronic archive
- The forum hall and rotating exhibition hall


## 5. Important facts
- Designed by Palestinian architect Ja'far Touqan, the museum stands on a 1,350-square-metre plot and has about 2,600 square metres of built area, according to the museum. Four ascending ramps form the main exhibition route, followed by a bridge to the historic Muqata'a building. The museum says the preserved headquarters area is presented as it was when Arafat left after a 34-month siege. Its Resource Centre includes a specialized library and a bilingual Arabic-English electronic archive covering Arafat and Palestinian history from the early twentieth century through 2004. The site also describes a 100-seat forum theatre and a rotating exhibition hall.

## 9. Visitor walkthrough
Read the museum as a sequence joining two buildings: the main exhibition space rises along four ramps, then a bridge leads toward the historic Muqata'a building associated with the former Palestinian presidency and Arafat's office and residence. The museum says the preserved headquarters area shows the conditions left at the end of the 34-month siege. Its design page gives a site area of 1,350 square metres and about 2,600 square metres of built space; the new building is clad in the Palestinian stone also used on the mausoleum, mosque and minaret. The Resource Centre includes a specialized printed library and a bilingual Arabic-English electronic archive whose documented coverage extends from the early twentieth century through 2004. The official site also describes a forum hall with a small 100-seat theatre and a hall for rotating thematic exhibitions. This is a guide to the documented design and facilities, not a claim about current opening or access.
