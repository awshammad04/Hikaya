## 1. Short overview
An oval mound north-west of present-day Jericho holding traces of human activity from about 10,500 BC, beside the perennial spring of ‘Ain es-Sultan.

## 2. Key historical timeline
| Period | Event |
| --- | --- |
| c. 10,500 BC | The earliest archaeological deposits of human activity on the tell. |
| 9th–8th millennium BC | A sizeable permanent settlement with a wall, a ditch and a tower. |
| Early Bronze Age | Archaeological material shows early urban planning. |
| Middle Bronze Age | A large Canaanite city-state with innovative rampart fortifications. |
| 2023 | Inscribed on the World Heritage List. |

## 3. Cultural and religious importance
UNESCO recognises Tell es-Sultan as exceptional evidence of the Neolithic shift to settled communal life and new ways of living from the land, of changes in social organisation and ritual — including the treatment of skulls after death — and of the rise of early urban culture in the Levant.

## 4. Main landmarks
- The Neolithic wall, ditch and tower
- The perennial spring of ‘Ain es-Sultan
- Early Bronze Age urban remains
- Middle Bronze Age rampart fortifications


## 5. Important facts
- The tell’s stratigraphy shows twenty-nine phases of occupation. By the 9th–8th millennium BC it was already a sizeable permanent settlement with a wall, a ditch and a tower. Middle Bronze Age remains reveal a large Canaanite city-state with innovative rampart fortifications. UNESCO inscribed the site on the World Heritage List in 2023.

## 9. Visitor walkthrough
The property is a single oval mound together with the spring beside it, which has watered the area for millennia. Its layers run from the Neolithic to the Bronze Age. The artefacts excavated here are no longer on the site.
